Where this started

A Whoop, a low HRV, and the first rule

In late 2020 my Whoop showed my HRV far below my normal. I felt fine and hadn't trained any harder. Two or three days later I felt rough, and it turned out to be COVID.

The band never said "you have COVID." It said something was off, and only I could work out what. Apple's hypertension notification looks similar from the outside: indirect signals, no direct reading. It is a different kind of signal, and the difference decides what this pathway is allowed to start from.

Decision 9 · the first rule

A pathway starts only from a signal that names one condition and has a test that confirms it.

The first kind is a "something's off" light: real, and for the member to act on. The second is a screening test for one condition, so it can route to one clinical action. The same line is why the app asks to read two things only: blood pressure and hypertension notifications.

The bar every idea had to clear

Cause a care event, or make one cheaper

Rezilient is paid a flat fee, so time in the app is a cost, not the product. A health-data integration earns its place only if it causes a care event, like a first visit, or makes one cheaper, like a dose change by message instead of a visit.

So the data alone is a gimmick. The care team acting on it is the product, and the app's job is to get trustworthy readings in front of that team at the right moment.

The pathway

Three ways in, one week, one care team

Every way in leads to the same week. The phone reads and checks. The server decides what counts. A person reviews it.

Way in A

The watch notices

Apple Watch sends a hypertension notification. Adults 22+, undiagnosed, iOS 26.2+, Series 9 or later.

HKCategoryType(.hypertensionEvent), read on the phone. Only { entry, date } is ever sent.

Way in B

Readings already on the phone

130/80 or higher on 2+ days in the last 30, from any source. An invite, not a diagnosis. Nothing leaves the phone.

historyScan { lookbackDays: 30, minDays: 2 }, the same goldens in Swift and Kotlin.

Way in C

A clinician starts it

During a visit or at a CloudClinic, dated to the visit, up to 14 days back. Consent happens in the room.

POST /v1/care/members/:id/enroll

  1. The member opts in

    "Start my check." Only readings taken after this moment are sent. Everything before stays on the phone.

    enrolledAt is the phone's read floor. The server refuses before_enrollment.

  2. "Do you have a cuff?"

    Yes: the week starts now. No: pick one up at a CloudClinic (that pickup is the first visit) or have one mailed. The week starts at the first cuff reading.

  3. A week of readings

    Morning (4 to 12) and evening (16 to 24), local time. 12 readings from validated cuffs count. Typed and cuffless readings are shown, labeled, and kept out of the average.

    protocol { days: 7, minReadings: 12 } · cuff allowlist by app + device model

  4. The phone syncs

    HealthKit and Health Connect are the outbox. The phone's place in them moves forward only after the server accepts a page, so nothing is lost offline.

    POST /v1/health/changes, idempotent on (platform, id), deletions as tombstones

  5. Rules in code

    Sort each reading (cuff, typed, cuffless, duplicate), slot it, average it, derive the state. The same rules are checked in Swift, Kotlin and TypeScript.

    server/src/rules.ts · goldens/*.json

  6. The care team reviews

    The member's own clinician, or for a first visit, one licensed in the member's state. They see the average, count, dates, sources, what was left out and why, and any very high readings. Never the raw stream. Then a visit, or a dose change by message, then a weekly check-in.

Safety, never an experiment arm

Any reading at 180/120 or above, from any source, shows a safety screen on the phone and creates exactly one task for the care team. Readings aren't watched live, and the app says so before the member connects.

The care team's side

How a member's readings reach the people in the web app

Rezilient's paramedics and clinicians work in a web app, not the member app. So the phone never talks to their systems. One service sits between them, and the care team hears about a member at a few moments, never as a stream.

Care team means everyone on Rezilient's clinical side: on-site paramedics, remote clinicians and coordinators. Clinician means the licensed physician, NP or PA who can diagnose and prescribe; a clinician reviews the week. Paramedics do the hands-on care and office readings at a CloudClinic.

  • Moments, not streams. Three things reach the care team: a very high reading (the on-call clinician, right away), a week that stalled after a nudge (anyone on the team), and a finished week (a clinician). Everything else waits for review.
  • The chart gets what a clinician has seen. Readings go in at review, and a very high one with its task. Unreviewed home data in the chart is the med-legal worry physicians name most.
  • Provenance travels. "Omron HEM-7155T via Apple Health, measured" reads the same for the member, in the web app, and in the chart (a FHIR Device and the source app).

Office and home, side by side

At a CloudClinic visit, the paramedic's connected cuff takes an office reading. Next to the home average, it shows white-coat hypertension (high in the office, normal at home) and masked hypertension (the reverse). An office reading alone misses both. My intake POC already builds a prep card for the paramedic and the remote clinician; the home average belongs on it.

The watch signal

What a watch notification means, and why it earns a visit

Apple's notification is a screen, not a measurement: "your pattern looks like high blood pressure, go confirm it." Apple sends the member to a cuff and to "your healthcare professional." Many working-age members don't have one. Rezilient is $0.

Who can get oneAdults 22+, not pregnant, no hypertension diagnosis. Every flagged member is undiagnosed by definition
Which watchSeries 9 or later, Ultra 2 or later. Series 12 still has no blood pressure sensor
Apple's next stepA cuff for 7 days, then "discuss it with your healthcare professional"
My estimateAt working-age rates, roughly half to 7 in 10 flagged members have hypertension
Notification → invite
"Do you have a cuff?"
Yes: start the week now → review → a visit if needed
No: a $0 visit first. CloudClinic reading + cuff in hand, or telehealth + cuff mailed → the week → review
  • A notification is enough to offer a visit before any cuff. It answers Apple's "talk to your doctor," removes the cuff barrier, and is the activation metric itself.
  • The visit doesn't replace the week. A diagnosis needs readings taken outside the office, so visit first only reorders it.
  • No notification never means normal. It misses about 6 in 10. No copy ever says "your watch says you're fine."

Cuffless is moving. Two devices that give numbers are now FDA-cleared: the Hilo band (July 2025, calibrated with its own cuff, writes to Apple Health with a subscription) and the Happy Ring (2026, no calibration claimed). Counting one is a single allowlist entry. It's still a clinical call: the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline says cuffless devices aren't for clinical blood pressure.

Rollout and measures

Build the end of the loop first

Data without a clinician acting on it does nothing, so way in C proves the loop before A and B send it more people.

PhaseShipsGate to the next
0 · about 2 weeksBaselines, answers to the questions below, pick the cuff and the clinical ownerProtocol signed off, review time agreed
1Pipeline and way in C on both platforms, CloudClinic cuff hand-out, a few clinics, behind a flagCompletion and review-to-action meet targets. The crisis path is drilled
2Way in B, staggered by employerFirst visits from B tracked against the staggered control
3Way in A, visit first (iOS 26.2+)Volume and conversion, reported descriptively: too rare for an A/B test

What it should move

  • First visits that started from an invite
  • Weeks completed (12 of 14 readings)
  • Readings received. HealthKit hides read denials, so "permission granted" can't be measured
  • Hours from "ready" to "reviewed"
  • Blood pressure controlled at 90 and 180 days

What must not break

  • The crisis path: never an experiment arm
  • Employer sees zero member-level data
  • Zero pathway events in third-party analytics. The funnel is computed on the server
  • Crash-free rate and battery no worse than the last release

Experiments change presentation only: invite wording, card versus card plus notification, reminder times. Thresholds, the crisis screen and what counts clinically are never an arm.
